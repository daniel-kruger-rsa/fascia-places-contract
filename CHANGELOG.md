# Fascia Places contract — changelog

Every published version of [places.v1.yaml](places.v1.yaml), newest first. A host vendors a
copy pinned to a tag and reads this to decide whether a diff needs its attention.
`scripts/publish-contract.sh` refuses to publish a version that has no entry here.

**"Editorial" means prose only.** A change to any schema node — a type, a nullable, an enum, a required list — is never editorial, however little the wire moves, and gets named on its own line here. We broke that rule on `0.17.1`, and a host caught it by diffing rather than taking our word: a document that does not say what is true has a reader who acts on it anyway.

| `0.31.0` | 2026-10-30 | Three additions, all additive on the wire. **(1) An `Attribution` entry gains `layers`** — which of this generation's layers that rights holder supplied, naming the same layers `/v1/places/generations` lists. Always present, never empty, sorted. **One entry per rights holder, not per layer:** a source supplying two layers appears once with both named, so South Africa's OpenStreetMap credit reads `["lines_streets", "places_osm"]` — measured on the real pack, not the fixture. **This includes layers whose credit lives in our API rather than in the pack**: streets and buildings were credited on the responses that draw them and were missing from `/v1/places/generation`, the one place a host reads its duty once at boot. The duty is now read from what the pack holds rather than from what its provenance records. **(2) A layer on `/v1/places/layers` gains `vintage_source`** — where that date came from. **Its first word is `read` or `asserted`, and that word is the promise**: `read` where the date came out of the source itself, `asserted` where we are naming the release we were given, then the evidence in prose. Present whenever `vintage` is. **Match the word, not the punctuation** — most layers read `read: …`, but layers ingested before this field existed read `read from …` with no colon, and four of South Africa's ten do today. **(3) SCHEMA NODE, not editorial: `Attribution` now declares `required: [source, text]`.** Those two have always been present on every credit this API has served and nothing in this document said so. `url` and `license` are deliberately **not** required — a rights holder with no canonical URL is ordinary, and `required` cannot be withdrawn inside v1. | **Yes if you render a licence screen, and it removes work rather than adding it.** `layers` exists because `source` said who to credit and nothing about what they supplied, so a screen showing credits under a fixed heading had to assert what the heading meant — one host's read **"THE OFFICIAL BOUNDARIES"**, which was true of a census boundary and false of a volunteer map the moment a country carried both. **Asked for by that host on 29 October 2026 and confirmed by them the same day**, in these terms: their screen renders one card per rights holder, so one entry per layer would make them merge back into one card, re-deriving a field we had already computed. **Do not deduplicate credits on `source`.** Two entries can share a `source` and differ in `statement`, and both must be shown — a rights holder may approve different wording for different vintages, as Statistics South Africa's 2011 and 2022 census statements do. The host that asked for `layers` found its own screen collapsing on the name and drawing only the first of two, which is a rights holder owed an acknowledgement and not getting one. Deduplicate on the whole credit **apart from `layers`**, with the layer lists unioned, which is what this API does; keying on the whole object including `layers` hands you OpenStreetMap twice. **If you read the display duty from `/v1/places/generation`, re-read it at `0.31.0`:** that response named fewer layers per holder than the pack holds, and a licence screen built from it would have credited OpenStreetMap for South Africa's locality tier and said nothing about the street layer beside it. **Branch on the first word of `vintage_source` before you display a vintage to a person.** An asserted date can be approximate — the GeoNames layer's is the date we downloaded the dump, because GeoNames regenerates its country files daily and stamps no version in them, so it is good to about a day — while a Municipal Demarcation Board year is not approximate at all. Those were indistinguishable on the wire until now. **Nothing changes for a client that ignores all three**, and `required` changes no response: it lets you stop defending against a credit with no `source`, which is a defence at least one host had written because this document permitted it. |
| `0.30.1` | 2026-10-28 | Editorial. **`SP` means the finest official cell a country publishes, not "a subdivision of a settlement"** — stated because reading the code's English the other way is wrong in a way that costs a host. South Africa fills two place levels below ADM3, `mainplace` then `subplace` inside it; India fills one, the revenue village, which is therefore `SP`. **An Indian `subplace` is not the counterpart of a South African sub-place**: it hangs directly off ADM3, where a South African *main* place hangs, and its median area (4.81 km² over 44,262 cells) is larger than a ZA `mainplace`'s (3.23 km² over 13,698) and more than twice a ZA `subplace`'s (1.93 km² over 24,743). Also says plainly that **a country may hold no cell at a level and that is not a gap** — ADM4, MP, GN and ST are absent from the India pack — and that such a layer answers `200` with an empty array, with `/v1/places/layers` as the thing to read. Nothing on the wire changes. **In the same version, and the reason it is published on 29 rather than 28 October: South Africa now serves a `LOC` tier.** 9,215 localities in `stats-sa-2022`, from the same dated OpenStreetMap extract the streets layer already uses, **2,134 of them carrying `codes.same_name_as_parent`**. This is a pack gaining data, not a contract gaining a field — `LOC` has been in this document since `0.27.0` and the mark since `0.29.0` — but it is named here because `0.27.0` told you no pack held a locality and `0.30.1` was drafted saying South Africa held none, and a host that read either and stopped checking would now be wrong. `stats-sa-2022` therefore carries **two credits** on `/v1/places/generation`, Statistics South Africa's and OpenStreetMap's; see the entry for `0.21.0` on reading that array rather than its first element. **And the OpenStreetMap credit now reads `source: "OpenStreetMap contributors"`, `license: "Open Database License (ODbL) 1.0"`** — the `Attribution` examples in this document and in `API.md` said `OpenStreetMap` and `ODbL 1.0`, which is what we served until 29 October 2026. The two strings were changed in the code so that one rights holder is not named twice in a response that carries both OSM layers; these documents had not caught up, and a host generating a client from `0.30.0` got the superseded pair. Example values only — no schema node moves. | **Yes, if you search South African cells by name without passing `kind`** — and that is a change in what a call you already make returns, not a new field to ignore. `/v1/places/areas/search` has never filtered by tier unless asked, so a ZA name search now returns `locality` hits mixed in with the official ones where yesterday it returned none. **A locality carries `lng`/`lat` and NO `bbox`** (`0.29.0`), so a client that indexes `bbox` on every hit reads `undefined` on ground it has always read a box from. Two ways through: pass `kind` to get only the tiers you want, or branch on `adm`/`kind` per hit — and **prefer the official cell wherever `codes.same_name_as_parent` is set**, which is the whole point of the mark. **Also if you branch on tier depth:** logic of the form "register at `MP`, `SP` is too fine" **inverts between the two packs** — in India `MP` is empty and `SP` is the settlement, so that rule silently registers nothing for India. If you branch on `adm` for comparison only, nothing changes. **`examples.v1.json` now carries `locality_search_za`, generated from production**, so you can assert your transport against a real locality with your own key rather than against an example from a deployment you cannot reach. |
| `0.30.0` | 2026-10-28 | A `/v1/places/crosswalk` result may carry **`rejected`** — the candidates that crosswalk **considered and refused**, nearest first, each with `meters` and a `reason` of `ambiguous`, `outside_radius`, `outside_parent`, `no_radius`, `no_parent` or `parent_unresolved`. Present only where candidates were refused; omitted, never `[]`. It is a **separate array from `to`** and nothing in it is a successor. | **Only if you act on `unresolved`,** and then it is the field that makes acting possible. A bare `unresolved` invites whoever writes the screen next to go and fetch candidates themselves, unbounded — which the measurement rules out: the distance from a disappeared place feature to the nearest surviving one has a median that swings **27×** between consecutive years, so a radius fitted anywhere but the one place it was measured is wrong. The refusals now travel with the answer instead of being re-derived. **Asked for by a host on 28 October 2026**, in those terms, as an amendment to our own proposal — which had `unresolved` arriving bare. **Why a separate array rather than entries in `to`:** a refused candidate inside `to` is one forgotten filter away from a host re-pointing a member onto a cell the crosswalk declined, and `to` is the array every client already loops over. The shape enforces *do not guess*; a sentence in this document would not. **Read the `reason`, not just the distance** — a candidate 400 m away in the wrong parent and one 1.8 km away in the right one are different problems. `ambiguous`, `no_parent` and `no_radius` are the common answers rather than edges. Of 39,699 localities in the first Maharashtra pack, 6,622 have no official parent at all and only **282** have a parent whose class has a measured radius — and inside those 282 there are on average **8.8** other localities within 1 km of the same parent, because the parent there *is* Greater Mumbai, which is two villages. The nearest candidate was therefore the nearest of about nine, and `moved` is now issued only where it is at least three times closer than the runner-up: **243 of the 282 are `ambiguous`** instead. A locality crosswalk refuses far more often than it matches by proximity, deliberately. Nothing changes for any crosswalk computed before today — none of them refused anything, because none of them looked. |
| `0.29.0` | 2026-10-28 | An `/v1/places/areas/search` hit gains **`codes`** — the source's own identifiers, as `/v1/places/area/{area_id}` has always returned them. Always present, `{}` where a source supplies none. | **Only if you resolve localities, and then it is required.** A locality's identity is its `osm_type` and `osm_id`, never its name, and `codes` is where they are. It also carries **`same_name_as_parent: true`** where a locality's name matches its official parent's — measured at **18,672 of 39,699 cells (47%)** in the first Maharashtra pack, including 15,659 villages and 2,917 hamlets, so the duplication is the common case rather than an edge. Prefer the official cell wherever that mark is set: a government behind it and a code you can store beats a matching name. **Why this is a version rather than a note:** `/areas/search` is the only way to reach a locality, because `LOC` never answers `/point` — so the mark was put in `codes` on the reasoning that it needed no contract change, and `codes` turned out not to be served here at all. A mark a host cannot read is not a mark. Nothing changes for an existing client that ignores the field. **In the same version, an `/areas/search` hit for a point-geometry cell carries `lng`/`lat` and NO `bbox`.** The omit-rather-than-nulls rule widens from *no geometry* to *no extent*: a locality is a point, so the old rule returned `[x, y, x, y]`, which this repo's own suite rejects (it asserts `bbox[0] < bbox[2]`) and a host's pipeline drops a candidate on. **Yes, if you index `bbox` on a LOC hit** — read `lng`/`lat` there, the shape a GeoNames hit has always had. Nothing changes for a polygon cell. **And in the same version, prose only, `StreetHit.bbox` says what a degenerate box means for a street: it is always present, and `west == east` or `south == north` is a street drawn exactly north–south or east–west, which is its true extent and must not be rejected.** Named rather than left as editorial because it is the opposite of the area rule landing in the same version, and a pipeline that learned one and applied it to the other would drop real streets. The document had said nothing either way, which is the failure this contract exists to prevent. |
| `0.28.0` | 2026-10-28 | A crosswalk successor gains **`basis`** — `overlap`, `feature` or `proximity` — and it decides which number comes with it. **`share` is now present only on an `overlap`**, and a `proximity` successor carries **`meters`** instead. `feature` carries neither. Successors are ordered best-first by whichever applies. | **Yes, if you read `share`.** It used to be on every successor and is now on `overlap` rows only, so a client that indexes it unconditionally reads `undefined` on the other two — the same shape of change as `bbox` becoming optional in `0.18.0`. Branch on `basis` before reading either number. **Why it exists:** `verdict` says what to do, never on what evidence. A cell measured to overlap its successor and a cell that was merely the nearest thing to the old one could both come back `moved`, and those warrant different confidence — the host who found a community linked to a name-matched cell 1.7 km from its actual ground is the case in point. Needed now because the locality tier has **no polygons at all**: a locality is a point, so proximity is the only basis available to it, and a distance dressed up as a share would have been a measurement that never happened. Every row built before this version is `overlap`, which is the column's default — a backfill that guessed would have been worse than the column's absence. Spelt **`meters`**, as on a GeoNames hit; two spellings of one unit inside one API is the fault this field exists to prevent. |
| `0.27.0` | 2026-10-27 | `kind` gains **`locality`** at **`adm: LOC`** — an unofficial cell, named by OpenStreetMap and by no government. `/v1/places/areas/search` accepts `kind=LOC`, and `/v1/places/layers` catalogues a `LOC` row for every country. No pack holds a locality yet, so nothing changes on the wire today. | **Yes, if you place members in a city whose official cells carry no usable name.** Greater Mumbai's are `F/South - Ward No. 203`, so **0 of one host's 499 Mumbai register rows match an official cell by name, and never will** — the two naming systems do not intersect. The same bounding box holds 1,295 named OSM `place=*` features. **`LOC` never answers `/point` by containment:** a locality holds no polygon, so no coordinate is inside one, and that is the tier rather than a gap. A later version will have `/point` *offer* nearby localities as candidates with distances in `meters`, the way it offers streets within 120 m — settled on 27 October 2026, not yet built. **A locality hit will carry `lng` and `lat` and no `bbox`,** like a `GeonameHit`: a point's extent is zero, and a host that indexes `bbox` unconditionally would read `[x, y, x, y]`, which a pipeline is right to reject. A locality's identity is its OSM feature id, never its name: 39.5% of `same` crosswalk verdicts in the South African pack are renames, and a crowd-mutable label makes a poor key. Reading `kind=LOC` on a pack that holds none answers **200 with an empty array**, not 400, like a bbox over the sea — so you may widen a name search onto it before any locality exists. |
| `0.26.0` | 2026-10-22 | A `/v1/places/crosswalk` result gains **`from_name`**: what the cell was called under `from`, carried in the crosswalk itself rather than joined, so it survives the old generation being dropped. | **Yes, if you re-link from a crosswalk.** Put it beside your own name for the thing you linked. **`same` certifies that the ground did not change and cannot certify that the link was ever right** — a host re-linking two communities found one placed four weeks earlier on the cell whose *name* matched the community, 1.7 km from the ground it occupies, and `same` would have carried it forward at full confidence. We had advised scripting every `same`; that advice was wrong, and this field is what makes the cheap human check possible in one call. |
| `0.25.0` | 2026-10-22 | `GET /v1/places/layers` now honours **`generation`**, like every other read. It accepted the parameter and ignored it. | **Yes, if you compare generations during a cutover.** This is the endpoint that says what vintage each layer is, and asked about a non-default generation it described the default instead — so a pack holding Census 2022 cells and the MDB's 2026 wards reported 2011 and 2020, identical to the live pack. Nothing was wrong with the data; the instrument was blind. Found by using it to check a real cutover, which is the only way a fault like this surfaces. An unknown generation is now `400`, as elsewhere. |
| `0.24.0` | 2026-10-22 | A layer on `GET /v1/places/layers` gains **`effective_from`**, and **`current` now accounts for it**: false before that date as well as from `superseded_from`. A published boundary is not yet a boundary that exists. | **Yes, if you branch on `current`** — and it is still the only field to branch on. It used to mean *not superseded*; it now means *in force*, which is narrower. The case that forced it: South Africa's ward layer is now the **MDB's own 2026 delimitation**, 4,485 wards taken directly from the custodian instead of 4,392 through a republished copy. It was downloadable in September and applies from **4 November 2026**, election day, so `current` is false until that morning and flips on its own. The cells are real and `/point` resolves them throughout — a layer that is not in force is labelled, never withheld. |
| `0.23.0` | 2026-10-21 | New endpoint **`GET /v1/places/crosswalk`**: what became of an `area_id` when the geography changed. Takes `from`, `to` and up to 200 `area_id`s, and returns **every successor with its share of the old cell**, plus a `verdict` — `same`, `moved`, `split`, `unresolved` or `unknown`. A generation pair with no crosswalk computed answers `404 crosswalk_not_loaded`. | **Only when you cut over**, and then it is the whole job. **Branch on `verdict`, never on the shares.** It returns a list rather than one id because one id is always available and often wrong: measured on Census 2011 against Census 2022 sub-places, 86.2% of old cells have exactly one successor, 13.8% split into two or more, and every single old centre falls inside some new cell — so a single-id answer would be well formed and wrong for one cell in seven, and you would store it without ever learning. `split` means a person decides. `unknown` is your id being wrong, which is deliberately not the same answer as `unresolved`, your cell having no successor. A `404` here is *we have not computed this*, never *nothing changed*. |
| `0.22.0` | 2026-10-21 | Every entry on `GET /v1/places/layers` now says **which vintage it is of** and whether that vintage still describes what is in force: `vintage`, `superseded_by`, `superseded_from` and **`current`**. Omitted entirely on a layer whose pack records no vintage. | **Yes, if you serve administrative boundaries.** One generation holds layers on different cadences from different custodians — South Africa's wards are a 2020 demarcation and its sub-places a 2011 census, both inside `mdb-zaf-2020` — so the generation name never told you, and nothing else did either. **Branch on `current`,** not on the two date fields: `superseded_by` alone does not mean a layer is wrong, because a census snapshot stays a true description of its own year. The case that forced this: South Africa's ward boundaries are superseded on **4 November 2026**, election day, when 4,468 wards become 4,488. Until now that would have happened with nothing in the data to say so — the same failure as a readiness flag that reports ready while nothing works. `current` is computed per request against the server's date, so it flips on the day without a deployment. |
| `0.21.0` | 2026-10-20 | **South African responses now carry a Stats SA credit**, and `GET /v1/places/generation` gains **`attribution`** — the display duty for that country and generation, read without a host key. The pack credit now also rides on `/geojson`, `/area/{area_id}/geojson`, `/area/{area_id}/neighbours`, `/harvest?summary=1` and `/building/{building_id}`, beside the layer credits rather than instead of them. | **Yes, if you serve South Africa.** `0.19.0` said nothing changes for a country whose source permits export, and that is no longer true of South Africa: Statistics South Africa supplied the acknowledgement they accept on 20 October 2026, so it is in the pack and in every response with room for it. Show `text` under a map and `statement` on a licence or about screen. Read it once at boot from `/v1/places/generation` — `/search`, `/areas/search` and `/harvest` without `summary=1` answer with a bare JSON array and cannot carry a credit, and changing those shapes inside v1 would break every client that reads them. **Correction to this repo's README:** it said Stats SA's conditions require that derivative products be vetted before publication. | `0.20.0` | 2026-10-16 | `kind` gains **`subdistrict`**, and the vocabulary is explicitly per country: a `kind` is the word a source uses for a level, and an `adm` code in a `kind=` parameter now resolves to whichever word that country's pack uses. | `0.19.0` | 2026-10-16 | A pack may **withhold its geometry**: where the source's conditions forbid redistributing boundaries, `/v1/places/geojson`, `/area/{area_id}/geojson` and `/street/{street_id}/geojson` answer **`403 geometry_not_served`**, naming the country and the source to credit.
